Drought cover that pays on the rain journal, not on a claim.

Pumpking is crop cover for smallholders. Cheap rain sensors keep a daily journal for each cell, and when the journal shows the policy’s run of dry days, the payout goes to the farmer’s wallet — no claim form, no inspector, no argument.

The policy

One cell, ninety days, fourteen dry in a row.

A policy names a cell, a window of days, a payout and a threshold: N dry days in a row. This one: cell 871e701b3ffffff, 90 days, 14 in a row, payout 1,000 mock USDC, premium 10 mock USDC.

0 actions

by the owner between buying the policy and receiving the money. When the journal shows the run, anybody can call settle_policy; the owner does nothing, and nobody can stop it.

When a day counts

A day is dry, wet, or not on record. Silence is not drought.

  • Dry — the day’s rainfall does not exceed the pool’s threshold.
  • Wet — it does.
  • No coverage — too few intervals had a value. A day without coverage breaks a run: a quiet network is never evidence of drought.

The run is the longest sequence of dry days inside the window, computed the same way in the program and in the interface — both run the same fixtures.

Who keeps the journal

The journal is kept by sensors anyone can run — and staked.

A phone can be a sensor: register it, stake, and it signs a rain reading on each interval. The cell’s value is the median of independent votes, so one sensor cannot write the day.

What it pays on

It pays on the cell’s rain, not on your field.

This policy pays on rainfall measured across cell 871e701b3ffffff, not on what happens in your field. Nobody comes to look at the crop, and there is nothing to claim.

Both of these happen, and neither is a fault to be fixed. The gauges measure a cell, your field is smaller than the cell, and rain does not fall evenly across either. It is what a payout with no claim form, no inspector and no argument costs.

Where this journal is kept

On devnet, with synthetic rain and a mock dollar.

The program, the signatures, the median and every transfer are real, on Solana devnet. The rain in the demo runs comes from scenario files and is marked synthetic wherever it is shown. Amounts are in mock USDC, a token with no value. Not audited.

Not here yet: a public trace from a reading to the payout, a methodology page, and a public weather reference to check the journal against — the next milestone. Thirty days of uninterrupted collection on the free plan is being measured now and is not claimed. The pool’s solvency under its whole book is a later milestone.
